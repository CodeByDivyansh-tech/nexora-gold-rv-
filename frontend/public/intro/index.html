MCX sells one metal in four contract sizes. Their prices should match, yet they drift apart. We asked whether those gaps are money on the table, and tested it on 15 years of official MCX prices, Apr 2011 to Oct 2026.

The problem

Commodity Derivatives Intelligence. GOLDM (100 g), GOLDTEN (10 g), GOLDGUINEA (8 g) and GOLDPETAL (1 g) all trade gold on MCX. Converted to the price of 10 g of pure gold, they should cost the same.

They don't, quite. So: when two of them drift apart, can you buy the cheaper one, sell the dearer one, and still make money after every cost?

GOLDM GOLDTEN GOLDGUINEA GOLDPETAL

The gaps are real

Each column is one trading week for GOLDM · GOLDPETAL, from 2011 to 2026: the biggest gap that week between the two prices and their normal level.

The red marker is one real trade: on 28 May 2014 the gap stood 189 bps from normal, the biggest the rule ever traded on this pair. It still lost ₹33,453 after costs.

Keep scrolling to travel through 15 years of it.

But every trade must clear a wall

Trading in and out of both contracts costs fees, taxes and slippage. A gap only pays if it beats the cost line: 48.8 bps.

For this pair, the gap cleared it on just … of days, the glowing columns.

So, is there free money?

No persistent edge after costs.

The gaps are real, but they don't pay. In the 2011–2023 holdout (18 Apr 2011 to 9 Oct 2023) the rule lost ₹1,30,655 over 88 trades after costs, though with zero slippage it would have made ₹51,534: costs decide it. It lost money in 9 of 12 years.
